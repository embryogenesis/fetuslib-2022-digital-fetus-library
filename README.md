## What this is

Forty-six pregnant phantoms, one per patient, each an MCNP input deck whose lattice card carries a voxelised abdomen: maternal organs, uterus, placenta, amniotic fluid, and a fetus with its own organ set. Verified against the bytes of Fetus_Model-34: a 194 x 132 x 236 lattice at 0.2 x 0.2 x 0.1 cm, spanning 38.8 x 26.4 x 23.6 cm, which is the maternal abdomen rather than the whole mother. The fetal labels run 110 to 131 and include 113 Fetus Brain, 118 Fetus Lungs, 119 Fetus Heart, 121 Fetus Liver, 127 Fetus UB and 128 Fetus Skin.

## Notes for anyone using this

- Why it is here: this is the only deposit the project has found that supplies BOTH a fetal body boundary and a fetal heart across the fetal period. The CRP pregnancy phantoms carry labels with those names and three of them are unusable — `Fetal Skin` there is a 457-voxel token mask, `Placenta` is absent at one of four stages and `Umbilical cord` collapses at term. This one has a real fetal skin label.
- And it settles what was wrong with the CRP labels. This deposit numbers 127 `Fetus UB` and 128 `Fetus Skin`; the CRP spreadsheet numbers 127 `Fetal Skin`. The two label sets are otherwise the same scheme, so the CRP name at 127 is an off-by-one against this one, and the CRP volume that measures like a bladder is a bladder. That was a hypothesis when it was noticed in the CRP mirror; it is now read off a sibling deposit.
- The format is an MCNP input deck, not a volume format. The lattice is a `fill=` card listing one universe number per voxel in plain text, which is why a 194 x 132 x 236 model is 20 MB and the set is 2.1 GB. Nothing in this project reads it yet. The parse is straightforward — the `fill=-96:97 -65:66 -117:118` ranges give the dimensions, the `px`/`py`/`pz` surface cards give the voxel size, and the universe numbers index the commented label table at the top of the file.
- Gestational ages are stated for 27 of the 46 models, not all of them. Table 1 of the paper runs from Fetus_Model-20 (17 weeks) to Fetus_Model-46 (35 weeks) and gives a real age in weeks, a fetal skull volume and an organ scaling factor for each. Models 1 to 19 carry no individual age: the paper says only that the cohort is 16 first-trimester, 12 second-trimester and 18 third-trimester patients, so models 1-16 are first trimester and 17-19 second, and an atlas placing those nineteen on a timeline has to say it is guessing.
- Models 27 and 40 are twins — two fetuses in one deck, each with its own skull volume and scaling factor (27: 55.25 and 70.59 cm3; 40: 307.33 and 356.84 cm3). A consumer that assumes one fetus per model will silently take whichever it finds first.
- Patient-specific, from clinical CT, and therefore a different warrant from both the CRP phantoms (reference models) and the KCL atlases (population averages). These are forty-six individuals. The organs, however, are NOT individually segmented: the paper states the fetal organs were scaled from an anchor phantom and placed by rotation and displacement, with only body contour, skeleton and brain segmented from the images. So the fetal *boundary* is patient-specific and the fetal *heart* inside it is a scaled standard organ put in a plausible place.
- Twenty-seven of the forty-six come with a published organ scaling factor, 0.76 to 1.68, which is the number the anchor organs were multiplied by. Anyone using the heart has to carry that factor with it.
